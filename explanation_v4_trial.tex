% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Overview}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The v4\_trial environment shares the same scenario setup as v3\_non\_changing:
a single ownship (slot~0) is controlled by the agent, all surrounding traffic
is uncontrolled, and the sector polygon, aircraft density, and spawning logic
are identical.
The fundamental change is in the \emph{observation representation}: the four
explicit per-neighbour CPA features of v3\_non\_changing are replaced by a
16-ray ego-centric LiDAR sensor.
The action space remains the same nine discrete ATCO-style clearances.

The motivation follows the approach of the \textsc{navigation-mappo-rl}
reference implementation, in which agents equipped with a LiDAR proximity
sensor and a navigation-progress reward successfully learned collision
avoidance in dense multi-agent scenarios.
Applying that representation to the BlueSky airspace environment allows a
direct comparison of the two observation designs under otherwise identical
conditions.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Environment Loop}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{\textsc{Reset} — initialise a new episode}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Generate random convex polygon (5--7 vertices)
\State area $\sim\mathcal{U}(10\,000,\,80\,000)$\,km$^2$,\quad
       density $\sim\mathcal{U}(5\,000,\,15\,000)$\,km$^2$/aircraft
\State $N \leftarrow \operatorname{clip}\!\bigl(\lfloor\text{area}/\text{density}\rceil,\;2,\;15\bigr)$
\State Compute $T_\text{max}$ from sector diameter and cruise speed
\For{each slot $s = 1,\ldots,N$}
    \State Spawn aircraft on perimeter arc $s$ with uniform jitter
\EndFor
\State Ownship $\leftarrow$ aircraft in slot~0 \quad\textit{(fixed for the episode)}
\State Compute initial LiDAR reading $\boldsymbol{\ell}_0$ around ownship
\State \Return observation $\mathbf{o}(\text{ownship})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{\textsc{Step}(action) — advance one RL step (30\,s simulated)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State Spawn any pending replacement aircraft
\If{action is heading offset $\delta$}
    \State $\psi_\text{cmd} \leftarrow \psi_\text{dest}(\text{ownship}) + \delta$
\ElsIf{action is Mach adjustment $\Delta M$}
    \State $M_\text{cmd} \leftarrow \operatorname{clip}(M_\text{cmd} + \Delta M,\;0.70,\;0.82)$
\EndIf
\State \textit{(Traffic aircraft receive no commands)}
\State Advance BlueSky simulation $60\times0.5$\,s $= 30$\,s
\For{each aircraft that exited the sector}
    \If{exiting aircraft is ownship}
        \State Apply exit penalty if $|\Delta\psi_\text{dest}| > 90^\circ$
    \EndIf
    \State Schedule replacement
\EndFor
\State Update ownship callsign if slot~0 was replaced
\State Compute LiDAR reading $\boldsymbol{\ell}$ around updated ownship
\State $r \leftarrow r_\text{safe} + r_\text{nav} - w_\text{work}\cdot c + r_\text{exit}$
\State \Return $\mathbf{o}(\text{ownship}),\; r,\;
               \text{truncated}=(\text{step\_count} \geq T_\text{max})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Step duration.}
Each RL step covers $60 \times 0.5\,\text{s} = 30$\,s of simulated time,
compared to 10\,s in v3\_improved.
The longer step ensures that a commanded 30° heading change is physically
complete before the reward is evaluated, so the policy observes the full
geometric consequence of each instruction.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Action Space}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Identical to v3\_non\_changing.
Nine discrete actions: six heading offsets and three Mach adjustments, applied
to the ownship only.
\begin{equation}
    \delta \in \{-30^\circ,\,-15^\circ,\,0^\circ,\,+15^\circ,\,+30^\circ,\,\varnothing\},
    \qquad
    \Delta M \in \{-0.04,\,-0.02,\,+0.02\}.
\end{equation}
Workload costs: 1.0 heading turns, 0.5 speed adjustments, 0 direct and hold.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{LiDAR Sensor}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The LiDAR sensor casts $N_\text{rays} = 16$ rays uniformly around the
ownship in its ego-centric frame (ray~0 is dead ahead; rays proceed
clockwise).
Each ray extends to a maximum range $d_\text{max} = 30$\,NM (six times the
separation standard).

Each traffic aircraft is modelled as a circle of radius
$r_\text{det} = 5$\,NM (equal to the separation standard) for
ray-hit purposes.
For a ray in direction $(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$ in the ego frame and an
intruder at ego-frame position $(p_x, p_y)$, the hit distance is
\begin{equation}
    t_\text{hit} = \max\!\bigl(0,\; t - \sqrt{r_\text{det}^2 - p_\perp^2}\bigr),
    \quad
    t = p_x\sin\theta + p_y\cos\theta,
    \quad
    p_\perp^2 = p_x^2 + p_y^2 - t^2,
\end{equation}
whenever $t > 0$ and $p_\perp^2 < r_\text{det}^2$.
The reading for ray $r$ is $\ell_r = \min_k(t_{\text{hit},k})/d_\text{max}$,
clamped to $[0,1]$, where the minimum is taken over all traffic aircraft~$k$
that intersect the ray.
A reading of 1 indicates no aircraft within range; 0 indicates an aircraft
whose detection circle reaches the ownship position.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Observation Space ($\mathbb{R}^{21}$)}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The observation has the same dimensionality as v3\_non\_changing (21 floats)
but a different structure: five ego features followed by 16 LiDAR readings
(Table~\ref{tab:obs}).

The LiDAR replaces the four explicit CPA neighbour features of v3\_non\_changing.
Rather than receiving pre-computed closest-point-of-approach distances and
lateral offsets for the four nearest aircraft, the agent receives a raw
geometric proximity snapshot and must learn to infer conflict geometry from the
spatial pattern of ray readings.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Reward Function}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{equation}
    r_t = r_\text{safe} + r_\text{nav} - w_\text{work}\cdot c + r_\text{exit},
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Safety term.}
The safety penalty is driven by the minimum LiDAR reading across all 16 rays,
converted back to nautical miles:
\begin{equation}
    d_\text{lidar}^\text{min} = \min_r(\ell_r)\cdot d_\text{max},
    \qquad
    r_\text{safe} = -w_\text{safe}\cdot
        \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_\text{lidar}^\text{min}}{d_\text{sep}}\right).
\end{equation}
This is the same exponential form as v3\_non\_changing but evaluated on the
current geometric proximity rather than the predicted CPA distance.
The penalty is always negative and approaches zero only when all intruders
are well beyond the detection horizon.

\paragraph{Navigation term.}
\begin{equation}
    r_\text{nav} = w_\text{nav}\cdot\cos\Delta\psi_\text{actual},
    \qquad
    \Delta\psi_\text{actual} = \psi_\text{dest} - \psi_\text{actual}.
\end{equation}
This reward is positive when the ownship is flying toward its destination and
negative when flying away, providing a continuous navigation signal analogous
to the progress reward in navigation-mappo-rl.

\paragraph{Workload term.}
$c$ is the discrete action cost, identical to v3\_non\_changing.

\paragraph{Exit penalty.}
$r_\text{exit} = -w_\text{exit}$ when the ownship exits the sector in the
wrong direction ($|\Delta\psi_\text{dest}| > 90^\circ$); zero otherwise.

\smallskip

The central research question of v4\_trial is whether a policy can learn
effective conflict avoidance from a LiDAR proximity snapshot alone, without
explicit CPA features in either the observation or the reward.
The agent must learn to infer the trajectory of surrounding traffic from the
spatial pattern and evolution of ray readings across consecutive steps.
All other design decisions are held constant to isolate the effect of the
observation representation.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Training}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Training uses the same PPO configuration as v3\_non\_changing: four parallel
environments (\texttt{SubprocVecEnv}), network $[128,\,128]$, entropy
coefficient $\beta_H = 0.02$, and all other hyperparameters unchanged.
Results are saved to \texttt{Runs\_saved/v4\_trial/}.
