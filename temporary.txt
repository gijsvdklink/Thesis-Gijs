================================================================================
STATISTICALLY SOUND TRAINING AND VALIDATION -- THE PLAN
Following Agarwal et al., "Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice"
(NeurIPS 2021), and the rliable library that comes with it.
================================================================================

The paper's message in one sentence: with a handful of training runs a point
estimate says almost nothing, so report INTERVAL estimates over RUNS, aggregate
with metrics that are robust and statistically efficient (IQM), and show the
whole distribution rather than one number.

Our current figures bootstrap over the 200 EPISODES of a SINGLE run. That
interval measures scenario noise only. It says nothing about whether another
training seed would have produced the same curve -- which is precisely the
question that made two earlier sweeps disagree by more than their own error
bars. Everything below fixes that.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. HOW OUR EXPERIMENT MAPS ONTO THE PAPER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    paper term      our experiment
    ------------    ------------------------------------------------------------
    algorithm       training condition: none / deterministic 30 s /
                    lognormal 30 s / probabilistic 30 s          (4 of them)
    run    (n)      one training seed: 0, 1000, 2000, 3000, 4000  (N = 5)
    task   (m)      one evaluation delay world: 0 s, 30 s, 60 s   (M = 3)
    score  x[m,n]   the MEAN over the 100 test episodes that run flew in that
                    delay world, for one KPI

The key structural rule: episodes are averaged INSIDE a run to give that run one
score, and every uncertainty reported afterwards is taken ACROSS RUNS. Episodes
of different training seeds are never pooled into one sample -- that treats seed
variance as if it were episode variance and shrinks the interval to a fiction.

Grid: 4 conditions x 5 seeds x 3 delay worlds = 60 evaluation runs, plus the
single no-CR reference. That is the 61 runs already listed in tutorial.txt.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TRAINING RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

R1  Five seeds per condition, minimum. If compute has to go somewhere, spend it
    on MORE SEEDS, not on more evaluation episodes. Episode noise is already
    averaged over 100 episodes; seed noise is averaged over 5. Ten seeds per
    condition would roughly halve the width of every interval below.

R2  Identical protocol in every condition: same timesteps, same
    hyperparameters, same --n-envs, same environment settings. Stop all runs at
    the SAME STEP COUNT, not at the same time of day, and record where each one
    stopped. Anything else measures the training budget instead of the delay
    model.

R3  Report the FINAL policy, not the best one. Picking the checkpoint with the
    highest evaluation score maximises over a small sample and is
    optimistically biased -- the paper shows this alone can account for most of
    a claimed improvement. The trainer already does the right thing: no
    best_model, only final_model, scored afterwards on the held-out pool.

R4  Freeze the protocol before the sweep starts. Once a KPI definition, a
    reward term or an environment setting changes, earlier runs are no longer
    comparable and have to be discarded rather than mixed in.

R5  Keep the scenario pairing across conditions (same seed -> same episodes).
    It costs nothing and makes the per-seed differences in section 5 much
    tighter. Independence still holds ACROSS seeds, which is what the bootstrap
    needs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. VALIDATION PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

V1  Held-out scenarios only: TEST_SEEDS, never touched during training.

V2  The same evaluation seed (--seed 1) and the same episode count for every
    policy and every delay world, so all 60 evaluation runs fly the SAME
    scenario sequence. Policy actions deterministic.

V3  100 episodes per (seed, condition, delay world). One CSV per evaluation
    run, named so the seed is machine-readable:

        seed_study/results/curve_<cond>_<delay>s_seed<K>.csv

V4  One no-CR reference per delay world, flown on the same scenarios. It is the
    anchor for normalisation in section 4, so it is needed at every delay
    level, not only at 0 s.

V5  Always pass an explicit --out path. A bare name resolves into the shared
    default folder and silently overwrites the previous sweep.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. AGGREGATION -- WHAT TO COMPUTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One KPI at a time. Never average a LoS rate with an exit deviation.

Normalisation is needed only when aggregating ACROSS delay worlds, because a
KPI means something different at 0 s and at 60 s:

    lower is better        normalised = (no_CR - score) / (no_CR - ideal)
    (LoS rate, exit deviation, advisories)

    higher is better       normalised = (score - no_CR) / (ideal - no_CR)
    (arrival rate)

with no_CR the reference of that same delay world (normalised score 0) and
ideal the physical best case (0 LoS, 0 NM deviation, arrival rate 1). Higher is
then always better and 1 is the target the optimality gap measures against.

Report, per KPI:

    (a) PER DELAY WORLD, in raw units, for the degradation curves:
        the mean over the 5 seeds with a 95% PERCENTILE BOOTSTRAP interval
        resampled over the 5 SEEDS, not over episodes.

    (b) AGGREGATED OVER THE THREE DELAY WORLDS, on normalised scores:
        IQM (interquartile mean: the mean of the middle 50% of the 15
        run-scores) with a 95% stratified bootstrap CI -- resample seeds with
        replacement independently within each delay world. Add the OPTIMALITY
        GAP, i.e. how far the condition stays below a normalised score of 1.
        Mean and median are dropped: the mean chases a single bad seed, the
        median wastes the few runs we have.

    (c) PERFORMANCE PROFILE: the fraction of the 15 run-scores above tau, swept
        over tau, with pointwise 95% bootstrap bands, one curve per condition.
        This is the figure that shows whether a condition is uniformly better
        or only better on average.

    (d) PROBABILITY OF IMPROVEMENT P(X > Y) with its interval, for each
        head-to-head claim actually made in the text (delay-trained vs.
        baseline, lognormal vs. deterministic).

Caveat to state in the report: with N = 5 runs bootstrap intervals are somewhat
optimistic -- the paper measures under-coverage below roughly 10 runs. Keep the
claims correspondingly tempered.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. COMPARING TWO CONDITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not draw conclusions from two intervals overlapping or not overlapping.
Build the interval for the DIFFERENCE:

    per KPI, per delay world, per seed k:

        d_k = score(condition A, seed k) - score(condition B, seed k)

Because the seeds are paired (R5), d_k removes the scenario luck the two
conditions share. Bootstrap the 5 differences and report the mean difference
with its 95% interval. If that interval contains 0, the honest sentence is "no
difference we can resolve with 5 seeds", not "no difference".

No p-values and no significance tests. Intervals and effect sizes only.

6. TOOLING -- WHAT TO BUILD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    pip install rliable

A new script, Validation/aggregate.py, replacing the episode-level bootstrap
that now sits in Validation/summarise.py:

    step 1  glob seed_study/results/curve_<cond>_<delay>s_seed<K>.csv
    step 2  per file, take the mean of the KPI column          -> one score
    step 3  per condition, build an array of shape (5 seeds, 3 delay worlds)
    step 4  normalise against the no-CR reference (section 4)
    step 5  hand the arrays to rliable

Sketch:

    from rliable import library as rly, metrics

    scores = {'none': A, 'deterministic 30 s': B,
              'lognormal 30 s': C, 'probabilistic 30 s': D}    # each (5, 3)

    iqm = lambda x: np.array([metrics.aggregate_iqm(x[..., m])
                              for m in range(x.shape[-1])])
    point, interval = rly.get_interval_estimates(scores, iqm, reps=50_000)

    profiles = rly.create_performance_profile(scores, np.linspace(0, 1.5, 100))
    prob     = rly.get_probability_of_improvement(
                   {'lognormal 30 s, none': (C, A)}, reps=10_000)

Changes to the existing code:

    - Validation/summarise.py: keep mean_and_ci as a single-run diagnostic, but
      the report figures must call the run-level version.
    - Validation/plot_report.py: read the per-seed CSVs, average the episodes
      within each run, and draw the band from the seed-level bootstrap. The
      curve stays the mean over seeds; only the band changes meaning.
    - Keep every per-episode CSV. They are the raw material for any later
      analysis, and the paper asks explicitly that all individual runs be kept.
