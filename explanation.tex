\section{Overview}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The environment simulates a single upper-airspace sector of approximately
80\,000\,km$^2$, populated by $N \approx 10$ A320 aircraft at FL350 and
450\,kts, where $N$ is determined by a traffic density of 8\,000\,km$^2$
per aircraft.
A single agent represents the ATCO: it selects one aircraft per step (the
\textit{focus aircraft}, chosen by maximum urgency) and issues it a heading
instruction.
Each RL step corresponds to 10\,s of simulated flight time (20 BlueSky
sub-steps of 0.5\,s each).
At the standard turn rate of 3$^\circ$/s, a 30$^\circ$ heading change
therefore completes within a single step, so issued actions take full effect
before the next observation arrives.

The two ATCO objectives are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Safety} — maintain at least 5\,NM horizontal separation.
    \item \textbf{Efficiency} — keep aircraft on their direct-to-destination headings.
\end{enumerate}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Urgency Matrix}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A symmetric urgency matrix $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ is rebuilt
from the aircraft's \textit{current} positions and headings at every step.
The entry $u_{ij}$ quantifies conflict severity:
\begin{equation}
    u_{ij} =
    \begin{cases}
        1 + 9\!\left(1 - \dfrac{d_{ij}}{d_\text{sep}}\right)
            & d_{ij} < d_\text{sep} \quad\text{(active LoS)} \\[6pt]
        \dfrac{1}{\max(t_\text{CPA}^{ij},\,1)}
            & d_\text{CPA}^{ij} < d_\text{sep},\;0 \leq t_\text{CPA}^{ij} \leq H \\[6pt]
        0   & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:urgency}
\end{equation}
where $d_\text{sep}=5\,\text{NM}$ and $H=600\,\text{s}$.
The formula is continuous at the LoS boundary (both branches equal~1 when
$d_{ij}=d_\text{sep}$).

The focus aircraft is $f = \arg\max_i \max_{j\neq i}\,u_{ij}$.
When $\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{0}$, focus falls back to the aircraft with the
greatest commanded-heading deviation from its destination bearing.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Action Space}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Six discrete actions set the commanded heading as an absolute offset from
the direct-to-destination bearing:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_\text{cmd} = \psi_\text{dest} + \delta, \qquad
    \delta \in \{-30^\circ,\,-15^\circ,\,0^\circ,\,+15^\circ,\,+30^\circ,\,\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}
The direct action ($\delta=0$) and hold action ($\varnothing$) are free;
resolution turns carry a small penalty $w_\text{act}=-0.1$.
Absolute offsets prevent unbounded accumulation and make action semantics
interpretable independent of history.

The hold action explicitly re-issues the current commanded heading to the
simulator every step, ensuring heading continuity regardless of BlueSky
internal state.
A mid-turn guard prevents \textit{direct} from overriding an in-progress
resolution turn (suppressed when $|\psi_\text{actual}-\psi_\text{cmd}|>5^\circ$).

\paragraph{Heading error (index 0).}
The signed normalised angle $\Delta\psi/180$ replaces the conventional
$(\cos\Delta\psi,\sin\Delta\psi)$ two-feature encoding.
Because $\Delta\psi$ is already constrained to $[-180^\circ,180^\circ]$ by
the wrap operation, there is no discontinuity to protect against, and the
sign directly encodes the required turn direction.

\paragraph{Deviation magnitude (index 1).}
A separate magnitude feature $|\Delta\psi|/30$ informs the agent how far
the aircraft is currently off its direct track, normalised to the maximum
action offset of $30^\circ$.

\paragraph{Clearance timer (index 2).}
This feature encodes how many steps have elapsed since the focus aircraft
last had urgency greater than zero.
A value near zero means a conflict resolved very recently; resuming direct
routing may recreate it.
A value near one means the aircraft has been clear for at least 30 steps
($\approx 5\,\text{min}$) and direct routing is safe.

\paragraph{CPA side (intruder index $+2$).}
The lateral offset of the intruder relative to the ownship at the closest
point of approach is
\begin{equation}
    r^\text{right}_\text{CPA}
        = \bigl(\Delta x_\text{CPA}\cos\psi - \Delta y_\text{CPA}\sin\psi\bigr),
    \qquad
    \begin{pmatrix}\Delta x_\text{CPA}\\\Delta y_\text{CPA}\end{pmatrix}
        = \begin{pmatrix}\Delta x\\\Delta y\end{pmatrix}
        + t_\text{CPA}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta v_e\\\Delta v_n\end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:cpa}
\end{equation}
A positive value indicates that the intruder passes to the right of the
ownship at CPA, meaning a left turn ($\delta < 0$) increases separation;
a negative value implies the opposite.
This feature directly encodes the required turn direction, removing the need
for the agent to infer geometry from Cartesian coordinates.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Reward Function}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The reward is a pure-penalty formulation:
\begin{equation}
    r_t = \underbrace{w_u\,\Sigma_t}_{\text{safety}}
        + \underbrace{w_d\cdot\frac{1-\cos\Delta\psi_f}{2}}_{\text{efficiency}}
        + r^\text{act}_t + r^\text{exit}_t,
    \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where $\Sigma_t = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}u_{ij}$ is the total urgency sum
and $\Delta\psi_f$ is the heading offset of the focus aircraft from its
destination bearing.
All terms are non-positive.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Reward weights.}
\label{tab:weights}
\smallskip
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
$w_u$           & Safety: total urgency sum      & $-3$   \\
$w_d$           & Efficiency: heading drift      & $-1$   \\
$w_\text{act}$  & Workload: resolution turn      & $-0.1$ \\
$w_\text{exit}$ & Wrong-direction sector exit    & $-20$  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The urgency sum $\Sigma_t$ scales automatically with conflict severity:
a pair at active LoS contributes $-3 \times 5 = -15$ per step,
while a pair predicted to conflict in 60\,s contributes approximately
$-3/60 \approx -0.05$ per step.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Training}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The policy is trained with PPO.
Observations and rewards are normalised online by a \texttt{VecNormalize}
wrapper (clip $\pm10$).
