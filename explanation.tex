\section{Overview}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The environment simulates a single upper-airspace sector of approximately
80\,000\,km$^2$, roughly the scale of Dutch upper airspace (ACC\,EHAA).
The sector shape is a random convex polygon with 5--7 vertices, resampled each
episode and filtered to a minimum isoperimetric ratio of 0.65 to avoid
degenerate shapes.
Traffic density varies per episode: $N \in [8,\,13]$ A320 aircraft at FL350
and 450\,kts, drawn from a uniform density of
6\,000--10\,000\,km$^2$ per aircraft.

\paragraph{Initialisation.}
All $N$ aircraft spawn simultaneously at episode start.
The sector perimeter is divided into $N$ equal arcs; each aircraft is placed
within its dedicated arc with uniform jitter, and heads toward a reference point
on the opposite side of the polygon (with $\pm30\%$ jitter in arc position),
then continues to a destination 2$\times$ the sector diameter beyond the boundary.
This guarantees realistic crossing traffic from the first step.
Minimum inter-aircraft spawn separation is $d_\text{sep} + d_\text{buf} = 10$\,NM.
When an aircraft exits the sector it is replaced within one step from a randomly
selected boundary arc, maintaining constant density throughout the episode.

\paragraph{Decision cycle.}
A single agent represents the ATCO.
Each RL step (10\,s of simulated flight, 20 BlueSky sub-steps of 0.5\,s each)
the agent issues one absolute heading command to the \textit{focus aircraft}
selected by the urgency matrix (Section~\ref{sec:urgency}).
At the standard A320 bank angle, a 30$^\circ$ heading change completes in
roughly 10\,s, so issued commands take full physical effect within one step.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Urgency Matrix and Focus Selection}
\label{sec:urgency}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Urgency scores}

A symmetric urgency matrix $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ is rebuilt
from scratch each step using only current positions and headings — no BlueSky
conflict-detection module is used anywhere.
The entry $u_{ij}$ quantifies conflict severity for pair $(i,j)$:

\begin{equation}
    u_{ij} =
    \begin{cases}
        1 + 9\!\left(1 - \dfrac{d_{ij}}{d_\text{sep}}\right)
            & d_{ij} < d_\text{sep} \quad\text{(active LoS, } u_{ij} \in [1,10]\text{)} \\[10pt]
        \dfrac{T_\text{warn} - t_\text{CPA}^{ij}}{T_\text{warn}}
            & d_\text{CPA}^{ij} < d_\text{sep},\;
              0 \leq t_\text{CPA}^{ij} \leq T_\text{warn} \\[8pt]
        0   & \text{otherwise (diverging or out of horizon)}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:urgency}
\end{equation}

where $d_\text{sep}=5$\,NM, $T_\text{warn}=180$\,s, and $t_\text{CPA}^{ij}$ is
the analytical time to closest point of approach computed from current positions
and headings assuming straight-line flight at 450\,kts.

\paragraph{Design rationale.}
The predicted-conflict branch is a linear early-warning ramp that rises from~0
at $t_\text{CPA} = T_\text{warn}$ to~1 at $t_\text{CPA}=0$.
The previous formulation ($1/\max(t_\text{CPA},1)$) was hyperbolic: a conflict
90\,s out yielded urgency $\approx 0.011$, far below the $w_\text{eff}=0.20$
efficiency pull, so the agent was not rewarded for acting preventively.
The ramp ensures a 90\,s conflict produces urgency 0.5, which clearly
outweighs the drift penalty and motivates early intervention.
Both branches are continuous at the separation boundary (both equal~1 when
$d_{ij}=d_\text{sep}$), and LoS urgency ($[1,10]$) strictly dominates predicted
conflicts ($[0,1]$), preserving the priority ordering in focus selection.

\subsection{Focus selection}

The focus aircraft receives the heading instruction each step.
It is selected as the aircraft with the highest row-maximum:
\begin{equation}
    f = \arg\max_i \;\max_{j\neq i}\; u_{ij}.
\end{equation}
When $\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{0}$ (no conflicts anywhere), focus falls back to
the aircraft with the greatest commanded-heading deviation from its destination,
measured as $(1-\cos\Delta\psi)/2$.

\paragraph{Hysteresis and sticky focus.}
Two mechanisms prevent rapid oscillation.
First, a 5\,\% hysteresis margin suppresses switching when the urgency
difference is marginal: the focus changes only if the new candidate's row-max
exceeds the current aircraft's row-max by at least 5\,\%.
Second, \textit{sticky focus} holds the same aircraft for a minimum of
$k_\text{sticky}=3$ steps (30\,s) before any switch is allowed.
The sole exception is an emergency override: if a new LoS appears
(urgency $>1$) while the current focus aircraft is clear (urgency $\leq 1$),
the switch occurs immediately regardless of the step counter.
These two mechanisms together stabilise the observation stream, preventing the
consecutive observations from being drawn from different aircraft perspectives
and thus giving PPO consistent rollouts to learn from.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Action Space}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Six discrete actions set the commanded heading as an absolute offset from
the current direct-to-destination bearing:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_\text{cmd} = \psi_\text{dest} + \delta, \qquad
    \delta \in \{-30^\circ,\,-15^\circ,\,0^\circ,\,+15^\circ,\,+30^\circ,\,\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}
The direct action ($\delta=0^\circ$) points the aircraft straight toward its
destination and is free (no workload cost).
The hold action ($\varnothing$) re-issues the current commanded heading to
the simulator every step without modification, also free.
Resolution turns ($\delta \neq 0$) carry a small workload cost $w_\text{work}$.

Absolute offsets from the \textit{current} destination bearing are recomputed
each step from the aircraft's actual position, so no heading error accumulates
across steps: \textit{direct} always means ``fly to destination now,''
regardless of prior deviations.
The maximum offset of $\pm30^\circ$ gives the agent enough authority to
resolve head-on conflicts without excessive off-track deviation.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Observation Space ($\mathbb{R}^{21}$)}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The observation is constructed ego-centrically from the focus aircraft's
perspective every step after the single focus selection call.
All features are dimensionless and bounded; intruder slots are sorted by
urgency descending so the most dangerous threat is always in slot~0.

\paragraph{Heading error as unit vector (indices 0--1).}
The conventional single-angle encoding $\Delta\psi/180$ is monotone but
discontinuous at $\pm180^\circ$.
The unit-vector encoding $(\sin\Delta\psi,\cos\Delta\psi)$ is smooth everywhere
and encodes both the required turn direction and magnitude without any
normalisation ambiguity.

\paragraph{Turn progress (index 2).}
At the standard A320 turn rate ($\approx3^\circ$/s), a 30$^\circ$ command takes
$\approx10$\,s to complete — exactly one step.
Without this feature the observation is aliased: issuing \textit{direct} while
the aircraft is mid-turn looks identical to issuing it when already aligned,
yet the correct response is completely different (wait vs.\ snap back now).
Turn progress makes the mid-turn state explicit and removes this aliasing.

\paragraph{Clearance timer (index 3).}
After a conflict resolves, the aircraft is still geometrically close to the
intruder.
Returning to direct immediately risks recreating the same conflict.
The clearance timer counts steps since the focus aircraft last had nonzero
urgency, normalised by a 30-step ($\approx5$\,min) horizon.
A value near~0 signals ``resolved just now, wait''; a value near~1 signals
``clear long enough, safe to go direct.''
Without this feature the agent cannot distinguish the two situations,
so it learns a single conservative hold strategy rather than a time-aware one.

\paragraph{Time to exit (index 4).}
The distance from the focus aircraft to the sector boundary along its current
heading, normalised by the cruise speed $\times$ $T_\text{warn}$.
This informs the value function that the return horizon is shortening as the
aircraft approaches the exit, which is critical under the focus-switching regime
where the critic otherwise cannot distinguish a fresh entrant (long horizon) from
an aircraft about to leave (short horizon).

\paragraph{Forward / right unit vector (intruder indices $+1,+2$).}
A single bearing-sine encodes left/right position but is ambiguous:
an intruder dead ahead and one dead behind both give $\sin\approx0$.
Decomposing into forward ($\cos$) and rightward ($\sin$) components resolves
this: the forward component distinguishes the two cases and the rightward
component retains the lateral information.

\paragraph{CPA side (intruder index $+3$).}
\begin{equation}
    r^\text{right}_\text{CPA}
        = \Delta x_\text{CPA}\cos\psi - \Delta y_\text{CPA}\sin\psi,
    \qquad
    \begin{pmatrix}\Delta x_\text{CPA}\\\Delta y_\text{CPA}\end{pmatrix}
        = \begin{pmatrix}\Delta x\\\Delta y\end{pmatrix}
        + t_\text{CPA}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta v_e\\\Delta v_n\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
A positive value means the intruder passes to the right of the ownship at CPA;
a left turn ($\delta < 0$) therefore increases separation.
This directly encodes the correct turn direction without requiring the network
to infer it from Cartesian geometry.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Reward Function}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every component is normalised to $[0,1]$ and attributed solely to the
focus aircraft.
No global penalty is applied: the global sector state enters the reward
only indirectly through the urgency matrix used for focus selection.
The reward is purely non-positive with 0 as the unreachable ideal:
\begin{equation}
    r_t = -\!\left(
        w_\text{safe}\cdot d_a
      + w_\text{eff} \cdot\frac{1-\cos\Delta\psi_a}{2}
      + w_\text{work}\cdot c_a
      + w_\text{exit}\cdot e_a
    \right),
    \qquad
    r_t \in \bigl[-(w_\text{safe}+w_\text{eff}+w_\text{work}),\;0\bigr].
    \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Danger term — soft-OR aggregation.}
The danger $d_a$ aggregates the urgencies of all intruders threatening the
focus aircraft via a soft-OR:
\begin{equation}
    d_a = 1 - \prod_{j \neq a}\!\bigl(1 - \min(u_{aj},\,1)\bigr) \;\in [0,1].
\end{equation}
The soft-OR has two advantages over a raw sum.
First, it is bounded: a single severe conflict drives $d_a$ near~1, but adding
further moderate conflicts raises it smoothly without ever exceeding~1, which
prevents variance spikes when multiple conflicts coincide.
Second, the worst pair dominates multiplicatively: if one intruder has urgency
$u\approx1$ the product collapses to near~0 regardless of other pairs, mimicking
the ATCO heuristic of focusing on the most dangerous situation.

\paragraph{Drift term.}
The efficiency penalty $(1-\cos\Delta\psi_a)/2$ is evaluated against the
\textit{commanded} heading, not the actual heading.
This ensures the penalty is felt immediately on the step the action is issued,
rather than 10--30\,s later when the aircraft completes its turn.

\paragraph{Workload term.}
A small cost $c_a=1$ for any non-free action ($\delta\neq0$ and not hold)
discourages unnecessary deviations.
Free actions are \textit{direct} ($\delta=0^\circ$) and \textit{hold}
($\varnothing$); all resolution turns carry the cost.

\paragraph{Wrong-exit term.}
A sparse penalty $e_a=1$ fires when any aircraft exits the sector heading more
than $90^\circ$ away from its destination.
This is attributed on the exit step and is not accumulated per step.

The ordering $w_\text{safe} \gg w_\text{exit} > w_\text{eff} > w_\text{work}$
encodes the ATCO priority: safety first, then correct sector exit, then
efficiency, then minimal control workload.
Because the reward is bounded to $[\approx-1.25,\,0]$ per step, reward
normalisation in \texttt{VecNormalize} is disabled (\texttt{norm\_reward=False});
the returned value is directly interpretable on an absolute scale.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Training}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The policy is a two-hidden-layer MLP trained with Proximal Policy Optimisation
(PPO) from Stable-Baselines3.
Four parallel environments run in separate processes (\texttt{SubprocVecEnv});
observations are normalised online with running mean and variance
(\texttt{VecNormalize}, clip $\pm10$); rewards are not normalised because they
are bounded by construction.

Rollouts of $T=1024$ steps per environment are collected before each
gradient update, giving $4 \times 1024 = 4096$ transitions per update.
The rollout is split into mini-batches of 64 for $K=10$ epochs.
Generalised Advantage Estimation (GAE, $\lambda=0.95$) propagates the
reward signal roughly $1/(1-\lambda)=20$ steps into the future, sufficient
to cover the multi-step consequence of a heading command.

An entropy coefficient $\beta_H=0.01$ maintains exploration throughout
training, preventing premature convergence to a deterministic policy
before the agent has experienced the full range of conflict geometries.
