\section{Overview}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The environment simulates a single upper-airspace sector of approximately
80\,000\,km$^2$, comparable in scale to Dutch upper airspace (ACC\,EHAA).
The sector boundary is a random convex polygon with 5--7 vertices, resampled
each episode and filtered to a minimum isoperimetric ratio of 0.65.
Each episode contains $N \in [8,\,13]$ A320 aircraft at FL350, drawn from a
uniform density of 5\,000--10\,000\,km$^2$ per aircraft.
All aircraft cruise at $M = 0.78$ (approximately 450\,kt TAS at FL350).
Speed commands are issued in Mach to avoid the CAS-to-TAS ambiguity that
arises at high altitude.

\paragraph{Initialisation.}
All $N$ aircraft spawn simultaneously.
The sector perimeter is divided into $N$ equal arcs; each aircraft is placed
within its arc with uniform jitter and flies toward a reference point on the
opposite side (with $\pm30\%$ arc jitter), then continues to a destination
twice the sector diameter beyond the boundary.
This produces realistic crossing traffic from the first step.
Minimum spawn separation is 10\,NM.
Exiting aircraft are replaced within one step to maintain constant density.
Episodes last approximately 60\,min of simulated time (2.5 sector crossings).

\paragraph{Decision cycle.}
A single agent acts as the air traffic controller.
At each RL step (10\,s simulated, 20 BlueSky sub-steps of 0.5\,s), the agent
issues one instruction, either a heading offset or a Mach adjustment, to the
\textit{focus aircraft} selected by the urgency matrix
(Section~\ref{sec:urgency}).
Heading changes complete within one step; speed adjustments settle gradually
over approximately 60 steps (10\,min), consistent with cruise deceleration.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Urgency Matrix and Focus Selection}
\label{sec:urgency}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Urgency scores}

A symmetric urgency matrix $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ is
recomputed each step from current positions, headings, and true airspeeds.
The entry $u_{ij}$ for pair $(i,j)$ is:

\begin{equation}
    u_{ij} =
    \begin{cases}
        1 + 9\!\left(1 - \dfrac{d_{ij}}{d_\text{sep}}\right)
            & d_{ij} < d_\text{sep}
              \quad\text{(active LoS, } u_{ij} \in [1,10]\text{)} \\[10pt]
        \dfrac{T_\text{warn} - t_\text{CPA}^{ij}}{T_\text{warn}}
            & d_\text{CPA}^{ij} < d_\text{sep},\;
              0 \leq t_\text{CPA}^{ij} \leq T_\text{look} \\[8pt]
        0   & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:urgency}
\end{equation}

where $d_\text{sep} = 5$\,NM, $T_\text{warn} = 600$\,s (10\,min), and
$T_\text{look} = 900$\,s (15\,min).
The warning horizon is set to 10\,min to match standard practice, in which
conflicts are resolved 8--10\,min before closest approach.
This ensures a reward gradient is present from the moment a conflict begins
to develop, incentivising early rather than reactive intervention.
Both branches are continuous at $d_{ij} = d_\text{sep}$ (both equal~1 there),
and active LoS urgency ($[1,10]$) strictly dominates predicted urgency
($[0,1]$) in focus selection.

\subsection{Focus selection}

The focus aircraft is the one with the highest row-maximum of $\mathbf{U}$:
\begin{equation}
    f = \arg\max_i \;\max_{j\neq i}\; u_{ij}.
\end{equation}
When no conflicts are active, focus falls back to the aircraft with the
largest commanded-heading deviation from its destination,
$(1-\cos\Delta\psi)/2$.

\paragraph{Commitment rule.}
A controller typically resolves one conflict before moving to the next.
This is implemented as follows.
If the focus aircraft is in an active conflict ($u_f > 0$), it is retained
unless a candidate $b$ satisfies $u_b > u_f \cdot (1 + \theta)$, with
override margin $\theta = 0.10$.
If the conflict has just cleared ($u_f = 0$) but fewer than
$n_\text{clear} = 5$ steps have elapsed since the last nonzero urgency, the
focus is held to confirm resolution.
An emergency override applies in both cases: a new active LoS ($u_b > 1$)
releases the commitment immediately.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Action Space}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Nine discrete actions are available: six heading instructions and three speed
instructions.
Heading actions set the commanded heading as an offset from the current
direct-to-destination bearing:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_\text{cmd} = \psi_\text{dest} + \delta, \qquad
    \delta \in \{-30^\circ,\,-15^\circ,\,0^\circ,\,+15^\circ,\,+30^\circ,\,\varnothing\}.
\end{equation}
The direct ($\delta = 0^\circ$) and hold ($\varnothing$) actions are free.
Resolution turns ($\delta \neq 0^\circ$) carry a workload cost of 1.0.
Offsets are recomputed from the aircraft's actual position each step, so the
direct action always means ``fly to destination now.''

Speed actions adjust the commanded Mach cumulatively:
\begin{equation}
    M_\text{cmd} \leftarrow
    \operatorname{clip}\!\bigl(M_\text{cmd} + \Delta M,\;0.70,\;0.82\bigr),
    \qquad
    \Delta M \in \{-0.04,\,-0.02,\,+0.02\}.
\end{equation}
The upper limit of 0.82 corresponds to the A320 maximum operating Mach (MMO).
Speed adjustments carry a workload cost of 0.5, lower than heading turns
because they are less disruptive to aircraft routing.
Commanded Mach is reset to $M_\text{nom} = 0.78$ on respawn.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Observation Space ($\mathbb{R}^{21}$)}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The observation is constructed ego-centrically from the focus aircraft.
All features are dimensionless and bounded.
The four nearest intruders are included, sorted by predicted CPA distance
ascending so the geometrically closest pass is always in slot~0.
Empty slots are padded with $(1,\,0,\,0,\,0)$.

\paragraph{Heading error (indices 0--1).}
The unit-vector encoding $(\sin\Delta\psi,\cos\Delta\psi)$ is smooth and
unambiguous across all headings, unlike a single-angle normalisation which
is discontinuous at $\pm180^\circ$.

\paragraph{Turn progress (index 2).}
Without this feature, issuing the direct action while the aircraft is
mid-turn looks identical to issuing it when already aligned, yet the
required response differs.
Turn progress makes the aircraft's rotational state explicit.

\paragraph{Speed offset (index 4).}
The commanded Mach is normalised over the reachable range $[0.70, 0.82]$.
Without this, the policy cannot distinguish a slowed aircraft from one at
nominal cruise, even though the available speed actions differ.

\paragraph{Forward/right components (indices $+1$, $+2$).}
A single bearing-sine is ambiguous: an intruder ahead and one behind both
give $\sin \approx 0$.
The forward component ($\cos$) resolves this, and the rightward component
($\sin$) retains the lateral information.

\paragraph{CPA side (index $+3$).}
\begin{equation}
    r^\text{right}_\text{CPA}
        = \Delta x_\text{CPA}\cos\psi - \Delta y_\text{CPA}\sin\psi,
    \qquad
    \begin{pmatrix}\Delta x_\text{CPA}\\\Delta y_\text{CPA}\end{pmatrix}
        = \begin{pmatrix}\Delta x\\\Delta y\end{pmatrix}
        + t_\text{CPA}\begin{pmatrix}\Delta v_e\\\Delta v_n\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
A positive value means the intruder passes to the right; a left turn
($\delta < 0$) therefore increases separation.
This encodes the geometrically correct evasion direction directly.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Reward Function}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All components are attributed solely to the focus aircraft.
The reward is purely non-positive:
\begin{equation}
    r_t = r_\text{safe}
        - w_\text{eff}\cdot\frac{1-\cos\Delta\psi_f}{2}
        - w_\text{work}\cdot c_f
        + r_\text{exit}.
    \label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Safety: CPA-margin penalty.}
\begin{equation}
    r_\text{safe}
        = -w_\text{safe}\cdot
          \max\!\left(0,\;1 - \frac{d_\text{CPA}^\text{min}}{2\,d_\text{sep}}\right),
    \label{eq:rsafe}
\end{equation}
where $d_\text{CPA}^\text{min}$ is the minimum predicted separation across all
focus-intruder pairs within the lookahead horizon.
The penalty is zero beyond 10\,NM predicted separation, grows linearly below
that, and reaches $-w_\text{safe}$ at zero separation.
Because this term activates as soon as urgency is nonzero (at $T_\text{warn}
= 600$\,s), the reward signal is present well before any violation occurs.

\paragraph{Efficiency, workload, and exit.}
The drift penalty $(1-\cos\Delta\psi_f)/2$ is evaluated against the commanded
heading so that the cost is felt immediately on the step the action is issued.
The workload cost is $c_f = 1.0$ for heading turns, $c_f = 0.5$ for speed
adjustments, and $c_f = 0$ for direct and hold.
A sparse penalty $r_\text{exit} = -w_\text{exit}$ fires when any aircraft
exits the sector heading more than $90^\circ$ away from its destination.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Training}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The policy is a two-layer MLP trained with PPO (Stable-Baselines3).
Four parallel environments run in separate processes (\texttt{SubprocVecEnv}),
each seeded independently.
Observations are normalised online (\texttt{VecNormalize}, clip $\pm10$);
rewards are not normalised as they are bounded by construction.

Rollouts of $T = 1024$ steps per environment yield 4096 transitions per
update, split into mini-batches of 64 for $K = 10$ epochs.
The entropy coefficient $\beta_H = 0.02$ encourages exploration of the
nine-action space, including the speed actions.
The network is widened to $[128,\,128]$ hidden units relative to earlier
versions.
